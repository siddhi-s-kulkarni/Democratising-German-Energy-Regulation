Redispatch is a mechanism born out of the issue of not being able to transport generated electricity from one area to another. In Germany, classically a lot of wind electricity is generated in the north. However with a lot of industry and consumption being in the south this electricity needs to be transported from the north to the south. At certain times transmission capacity is not sufficient to carry electricity generated from the north to the south.

Using redispatch, grid operators curtail a generating plant before the bottleneck in direction of transmission to prevent harm to cables and congestion in one area of the grid. This leads to needing to ramp up a plant elsewhere to make up for the generation deficit.

### History
Historically, §13 EnWG covered conventional generation redispatch, while renewable and CHP plants were addressed through a related mechanism called Einspeisemanagement (feed-in management) under  §13 EnWG and in conjunction with §14–15 EEG and §3 KWKG. 
The stepping back from coal and the transition to renewable generation has led to increased feed-in and power flows in the grid leading grid operators to conduct redispatch actions more frequently. Also it became difficult to manage two different compensation regimes and logics. 

### Redispatch 2.0
The redispatch (Redispatch 2.0) as we know today was introduced on 01.10.2021. The old feed-in management rules for renewable and CHP plants were repealed and merged with conventional power plants into one unified congestion management mechanism under §§13, 13a, 14 EnWG. More specifically all plants >= 100 kW and also plants controllable by a grid operator were brought into scope.

This currently active redispatch mechanism compensates operators of plants when their plant is curtailed. This is essential since operators forego possible revenues that could be earned from the plant.

### Redispatch Vorbehalt
The "Redispatch-Vorbehalt" is a suggested revision introduced by the Bundeswirtschaftsministeriums (BMWE).

Since grid operators in regions with high renewable energy build up have to deal with high generation influx and the resulting congestion issues, a revision in the Redispatch mechanism has been introduced, to make renewable installation more grid oriented, which in practice discourages further build-up in these already congested regions. The pillars of this revision are:
- Grid operators can state that a specific region is "capacity-limited" if they have had to curtail more than 5% of potential electricity generation in the region.
	If a region receives this label of being capacity-limited:
	- It will remove the priority for the grid connection of PV and wind energy plants. Normally renewable energy plants have a legal priority claim to receive grid connections ahead of other types of plants. However, in a capacity limited region this priority would go away. This would mean less certain grid connections.
	- Plants installed in capacity-limited areas will be exempt from receiving compensation during curtailment for the first 6 years.

This suggested revision has received a lot of criticism for drastically reducing the bankability of renewable energy projects.
