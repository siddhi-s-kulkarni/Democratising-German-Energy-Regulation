Note: This is not part of a law. 

AgNes stands for "Allgemeine Netzentgeltsystematik Strom"

AgNes is a regulatory determination proceeding led directly by #BNetzA using its own independent regulatory authority, not a law requiring approval from the #Bundestag and the #Bundesrat . The #BNetzA opened this proceeding on the 12 May 2025 and it is moving through a structured public consultation process.

The current mechanism for deciding grid fees will cease to be in force from the 31st December 2028 (StromNEV).

Therefore a follow-up mechanism is necessary to succeed the StromNEV. The goal is to have this follow-up mechanism enable a fairer allocation of charges to all grid network users and also address the changing conditions due to the energy transition such as the key role played by prosumers and battery storage. Additionally the goal is to have this mechanism market-oriented and transparent. 

The #BNetzA described the current changing conditions of the energy transition as follows: Currently, all consumers carry the costs of the grid fees. However, the volume of grid feed-in is also increasing which is increasing the need for grid infrastructure build-up. There is an evident change in the consumption behavior of customers (all sizes) since they are increasingly self-generating. Especially heat-pumps and electric vehicle charging leads to higher consumption with a high simultaneity factor.

There have been several consultations starting in May 2025 for finalising this framework. The final follow-up mechanism as a result of AgNes is expected to be published by end of 2026.

Connection to [[§118 EnWG]]:
The 20 year protection from grid fees for battery storage systems commissioned before 4th August  2029 was briefly under threat under the AgNes scheme where the #BNetzA considered applying new rules retroactively to existing systems. However, an interim update on the 27th May 2026 confirmed that the protection remains and is tied to documenting a final investment decision before the new rules under the AgNes proceeding take effect and the battery commissioning still taking place before 4th August 2029.
### Proposed fee models

1. Battery storage (with its own grid connection): These have to pay an annual capacity fee which will be tied to the contracted connection capacity.

2. Co-located storage (with a common grid connection with a generating plant): A combined grid fee together with the generating source it is coupled to will need to be paid. Electricity drawn from the grid and later fed back out would be exempt from standard consumption charges as long as it is meterable separately.

3. Generation plants: Generation plants > 30 kW will owe a feed-in fee. This will be capacity based and based on a formula weighing grid costs against a plant's contracted feed-in capacity.

4.  Customer specific fee structure:
	1. Customers who are not prosumers stay in the current fee structure.
	
	2. Prosumers pay an extra 70-90% surcharge on grid fees because they are a prosumer and can also feed into the grid.
	
	3. For customers > 100000 kWh (see [[C&I Segment]]), a capacity based grid fee will be applied which has to be booked. If this booked capacity is exceeded a fine of 200-350% of the normal rate will be applicable.
	
5. Unlike the above fixed fee models, AgNes also introduces dynamic grid fees which are charges that fluctuate with time according to grid conditions. These will only apply to battery storage and generation and will not apply to regular customers. This is separate from and does not affect the §14a Module 3 [[§14a EnWG]], which stays in place unchanged.
