The backbone of the EEG is the #Einspeisevergütung (feed-in tariff) i.e. receiving fixed unchanged price for every kWh sold to the grid for a period of 20 years after commissioning the plant. This is a fixed feed-in tariff. Also the operator of the plant does not have to sell the electricity on his own but just feeds the electricity directly into the grid. This was first introduced in 2000.

### Direktvermarktung 
This can be translated to direct marketing in English  (in the sense of directly selling electricity on the power market, not advertising). Generating plants that do not participate in the fixed feed-in tariff  scheme are part of the direct marketing.
The #Direktvermarktung was introduced in the year 2012 as an option for all major renewable energy plants to integrate them into the power market. At the time of introduction, it provided the option to sell a part or all of the electricity generated by plants using Direktvermarktung. The types of renewable energy plants that fall under this scheme are:
- PV
- Wind energy
- Biogas and biomass
- Hydroelectric power
- Geothermal energy
In 2016/2017 it  became compulsory for all plants >= 100 kW to participate in this scheme. The requirement is tied to a plant's installed capacity.
#### Mechanism for #Direktvermarktung 
The operator of the plant sells the electricity from his plant on the electricity market via a Direktvermarkter (this is a separate company that performs the trading and can be thought of as the direct marketing service provider). There are three terminologies to be understood in this context and the image below explains the relationship between these terminologies:
- Anzulegender Wert
- Marktwert
- Marktprämie

If the Marktwert is below the Anzulegender Wert, the grid operator pays this difference to the plant operator as the Marktprämie.
If the Marktwert is above the Anzulegender Wert, the Marktprämie falls to 0 but the plant operator keeps all the sales.
The system is therefore asymmetrical. It guarantees a minimum but does not fix a ceiling (maximum) hence the profit for the plant operator can be very high.
This model is essentially composed of the moving Marktwert and a flexible Marktprämie and both these components add up to reach the goal of the Anzulegender Wert.
The Marktwert is paid by the Direktvermarkter to the plant operator and the Marktprämie is paid by the grid operator to the plant operator.

### Solarspitzengesetz 
The #Solarspitzengesetz which was finalised on the 25 February 2025 reformed the system of Einspeisevergütung (fix feed-in tariff). 
The changes essentially mean that Einspeisevergütung for generation is reduced to 0€ for negative day-ahead price hours. This is the case for all new PV plants >2 kWp  installed after the 25 February 2025.
However this doesn't mean that those negative price hours in that 20 year period are lost forever. These hours are added at the end of the 20 year period.
This also applies to the Marktprämie, which will be 0€ during negative price hours. 
The goal is to create incentives for generating plants to not feed into the grid so that the prices which are already negative do not become more negative. This would put the grid under additional stress.
The goal of the EEG through the #Solarspitzengesetz  is to achieve a more market oriented behaviour for PV generation of all sizes.

### Participation in Direct marketing by plant size
The current status of  generation level of plants and the necessary participation in the Direktvermarktung is as follows:

| **Plant size**               | **Direktvermarktung**                                |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **> 100 kWp**                | **compulsory**                                       |
| **25 – 100 kWp**             | voluntary _(expected to become mandatory from 2027)_ |
| **7 – 25 kWp**               | voluntary                                            |
| **2 – 7 kWp**                | voluntary                                            |
| **< 2 kWp** (balcony plants) | voluntary                                            |
## Upcoming developments: 
### MiSpeL : Marktintegration von Speichern und Ladepunkten
#MiSpeL  strives to include battery storage systems (more specifically behind the meter battery storage) and charging points in market participation. It replaces the Ausschließlichkeitsprinzip (exclusivity option) which allowed batteries to only charge from the renewable energy source installed on customer site if the customer wanted to receive a fixed feed-in tariff. Concretely this meant that before MiSpeL, plants with batteries and renewable energy sources (for e.g. PV) could only earn a fixed feed-in tariff on the electricity generated by the PV. Hence the batteries were only allowed to charge from the PV.

With MiSpeL, batteries can finally be operated fully bidirectionally and the customer can still receive a feed-in tariff. They can charge from the renewable energy source that they are paired with as well as from the grid. Similarly they can also discharge into the grid. 

The exact details for MiSpeL were to be released and enter into force by 30th June 2026. 
On the 5th August 2026, it was announced that the concrete details will release and come into effect on the 1st October 2026. Furthermore the #BNetzA also released the current working drafts of the regulation.
After final publication, there will be a transitionary period for affected installations.

### EEG_Netzanschlusspaket
Changes to the core system of the EEG have been suggested through a draft finalised on the 29 July 2026 through the "Netzanschlusspaket" in 2026. The changes have currently been finalised by the #Bundeskabinett and are currently being reviewed and consulted on by the #Bundestag and need to be reviewed by the #Bundesrat. Therefore these changes are not yet legally binding.

The background for these changes is very similar to that of the #Solarspitzengesetz . Since the EEG was introduced as an incentive mechanism to promote the build of renewable energies it has been very successful in the last two decades. Furthermore, renewable energies now form a substantial part of the electricity mix, and as electricity sources they have become cheaper and there are often situations where more renewable energy is fed into the grid than the grid can handle, which leads to grid congestion issues and frequent negative grid prices.

These changes have been introduced to make renewable energies more market and grid oriented. Another reason is that the current EEG funding scheme's EU state aid approval expires at the end of 2026 , meaning a replacement scheme must take effect by 1 January 2027 which is what is driving the tight legislative timeline.

The suggested changes entail that all new plants should be involved in direct marketing #Direktvermarktung regardless of their size and the previous limit of 100 kW should be removed. The changes also contain two cushion instruments: a) the temporary transitional payment instead of the permanent feed-in tariff and b) the direct marketing bonus for smaller plants.  These two mechanisms are sequential, not simultaneous. A plant first receives the transitional payment while it is not yet in direct marketing and only becomes eligible for the direct marketing bonus once that transition period ends and it actually begins direct marketing.

a) Transitional payment
PV systems commissioned from 2027 continue to receive a transitional payment from a grid operator for up to 36 months. This payment is calculated as #Anzulegenderwert - 1ct/kWh and the draft simultaneously lowers the Anzulegender Wert for solar to 6.2 ct/kWh which results in a transitional rate of 5.2 ct/kWh for plants commissioned by 31 July 2027. The rate is lower for plants commissioned later which is in line with the EEG's existing degression schedule. After this period they must participate in direct marketing.

| **Commissioning of the PV system** | **Transition payment possible for** |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 2027                               | Plants below 50 kW                  |
| 2028                               | Plants under 25 kW                  |
| 2029 and 2030                      | Plants below 7 kW                   |
| from 2031                          | No transitional payment             |

b) Direct marketing #Direktvermarktung  bonus 
In order to offset the additional costs for plants to start direct marketing, the draft contains a new direct marketing bonus. Plants below 25 kW receive a surcharge of 1.5 ct/kWh on the directly marketed electricity for a maximum of 48 months.

Existing plants will retain their guaranteed funding. The changes only affect plants to be commissioned from 2027.

In summary, the 25 kW limit is the real decider.
All plants must eventually participate in direct marketing. The transitional payments and the marketing bonus are just temporary  size- and year-dependent bridges (shown in above table). But what happens after those end depends entirely on plant size.
- Under 25 kW: They do not receive any ongoing Marktprämie once their transition period and marketing bonus expire. The grid operator continues taking their power but without compensation.
- Plants >= 25 kW: Once a plant is in ongoing direct marketing, its Marktprämie is calculated against a permanent Anzulegender Wert  of 6.2 ct/kWh. This is fixed and no longer varying by plant size as it did under the old system. 
	- Only plants  commissioned in 2027 and are also < 50 kW receive a temporary transitional payment first (see the transitional payment mechanism above based on 6.2 ct/kWh minus 1 ct/kWh).  
	- Unlike the under 25 kW group, plants in this category do not receive a direct marketing bonus. The bonus is intended to cushion the new compliance costs of direct marketing (metering, remote control hardware, and contracting with a Direktvermarkter) which fall disproportionately on the smallest plants since these are mainly fixed costs regardless of plant size.


There is also a permanent cap on direct feed-in for new rooftop PV plants under 100 kW: they may feed a maximum of 50% of their installed capacity into the grid at the connection point. This cap applies only to grid feed-in. Self-consumption and charging a battery storage system are unaffected.

For plants >= 100 kW the unidirectional system will become a bidirectional system. Until now in the #Direktvermarktung when the Marktwert is higher than the Anzulegenderwert,  the operator can keep all the gains. Under the changes in the EEG 2027 this system will become symmetrical where when the Marktwert is higher than the Anzulegenderwert, the plant operator has to pay back the grid operator. The repayment is oriented towards the spot price. The operator however always earns a minimum of 0.5 ct/kWh. To do this operators must register the plant within 6 months of commissioning. If the Marktwert is lower than the Anzulegender Wert, the plant operator still receives the difference from the grid operator.
