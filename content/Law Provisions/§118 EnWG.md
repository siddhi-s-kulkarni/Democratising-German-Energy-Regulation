Battery storage systems that go into operation by August 4, 2029, are exempt from grid fees for a period of 20 years under this paragraph in the [[EnWG]].  Concretely, no grid fees are currently charged for electricity that is drawn from the grid and later fed back into it. This is what the 20 year exemption actually covers.

Without this exemption, a battery storage system would be charged twice, once for drawing electricity from the grid and once for feeding it back, even though it is the same unit of energy and not creating new grid demand.

To protect **investment confidence**, a decision by the Federal Network Agency #BNetzA ensures that this exemption continues to apply to existing plants and projects with final investment decisions, even when new fee regulations take effect after 2029.

**New regulations from 2029:** The BNetzA plans to introduce a **capacity fee** instead of the current exemption for future storage systems starting in 2029, as part of the grid fee reform [[AgNes]].

Under the [[AgNes]] draft the capacity fee would work differently depending on the setup. Grid connected storage would pay an annual capacity fee tied to its contracted connection capacity at the same rate as the generation feed-in fee rate. Co-located storage would pay one combined fee with the generation source that it is paired with and electricity drawn from the grid and fed back into the grid would stay exempt from standard consumption charges as long as it is metered separately.
