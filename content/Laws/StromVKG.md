StromVKG (Strom-Versorgungssicherheits- und Kapazitätengesetz) which can also be translated as the Electricity Supply Security and Capacity Law was introduced in response to the growing share of renewable energy in the generation mix.  Due to Germany's stepping out of nuclear power and reducing share of coal generation, there are periods of low renewable generation where reliable available generating capacity is scarce.

This law was recently passed in the #Bundestag on 9th July 2026 and the #Bundesrat gave approval shortly after.

This law aims to establish a central capacity market which gives investors more predictable revenue and additional incentives for investment.

Three auctions will be conducted for procuring the required dispatchable capacity of 11 GW by 2031. This is necessary for ensuring security of supply.

The following are the requirements for the capacity that will be procured:

- Long-term capacity needs to deliver for 10 consecutive hours at 80% of nominal power. Energy-limited technologies can take 3 hour breaks before having to deliver for another 10 hours.

- The maximum bid was increased from €173,000 to €244,000 per MW of derated capacity per year. This was done to reduce risk of undersubscribed auctions due to additional investment costs and technical requirements that are also needed (for hydrogen technology as an example). Derated capacity here is a technology-specific weighting factor which reflects a plant's actual contribution to the security of supply, rather than its raw installed capacity.

The StromVKG is an initial step and a bridge towards a comprehensive capacity mechanism which has to be operational starting from 2032. It only covers a part of the future firm capacity that will be needed.

On September 2, 2026 the EU Commission approved Germany's StromVKG capacity mechanism which should cost between €15.6 billion and €35.2 billion in state aid. It funds gas fired plants, storage and flexible demand response. Any new gas fired plant bidding for a contract must be hydrogen ready. All supported capacity will be required to operate in a climate neutral way by 2045. The plants will be live and available for electricity delivery starting 2031.

The first capacity auction opened for bids on 8 September 2026 offering 4500 MW of derated capacity under 15-year contracts at the set €244,000 per MW ceiling. Awards are due by 3 November 2026. The auction was reported as significantly oversubscribed.
