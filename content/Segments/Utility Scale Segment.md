This refers to plants larger (in power and capacity) than the [[C&I Segment]]. There is no official limit in kW starting which a plant becomes of utility scale. However, commonly #Frontofthemeter plants in public spaces are referred to as utility scale plants.

The definition of what constitutes utility scale has been changing according to economies of scale. For example if 5 MW was considered as utility scale benchmark 10 years ago, it may now be said that 20 MW is the benchmark.

Utility scale plants could be wind farms, PV farms, battery storage or co-located plants. A co-location plant consists of more than one type of plant at the same location. This could be a PV plant and a battery storage at the same location which are essentially built to store the excess PV generated power in the battery and discharge the battery when the PV does not generate any power.
There are also concepts of PV and wind being co-located since these generating sources have complementary generation patterns across seasons and weather conditions.

The concept of [[Redispatch]] affects the bankability and development of utility scale projects since projects might get rejected by the grid operator and they also suffer from a volume reduction in earnings when they do not receive compensation during curtailment.
[[AgNes]] also affects the bankability of projects through payment of capacity based fees for battery storage and co-located plants.
